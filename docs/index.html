AdapterBench

Does the shape of a hypernetwork-generated adapter actually matter?

The question

Text-to-LoRA and Doc-to-LoRA have a hypernetwork emit a LoRA — but neither tests that choice of representation against alternatives under a fixed protocol. AdapterBench isolates it.

It holds the hypernetwork shell, training loop, data, and evaluator constant, and varies only the generated representation — the codec. Any difference in the scored result is therefore attributable to shape, not to a confound in the recipe.

Fixed substrate

  • hypernetwork trunk & conditioner
  • training data & loop
  • evaluator & its control

Varied — the codec

  • how many numbers the net emits
  • how they become a weight/activation update
  • e.g. LoRA, IA³, DoRA, full-delta…

Two active settings

Each fixes a frozen interpreter and the hypernetwork shell, and differs only in what the hypernetwork is conditioned on and how the adapter is scored. Every headline is matched − control, never raw performance.

Negative results are results

Several plausible settings failed the same controls that validate T2L and D2L. Standard input-visible Text-to-LoRA did not reliably beat junk descriptions; prompt-conditioned image tilting did not beat shuffled prompts; selective erasure became broad suppression; and a reference-image identity oracle remained at chance.

These experiments are excluded from the active benchmark surface, but their measurements and failure modes are preserved in NEGATIVE_RESULTS.md. The record distinguishes a failed shuffle audit from a failed capacity gate and from a method that had no per-instance condition to shuffle.

Leaderboards

One board per setting. Each entry is a codec at its own best free hyperparameters (scale, lr, warmup, steps), over ≥3 seeds where noted; the shared substrate and the shape-identity hyperparameter (rank) are fixed. Settings are never pooled — the cross-setting question is whether a codec's relative behavior repeats.

Add a codec

A new adapter shape needs two things: an output structure (how many numbers the hypernetwork emits and how they become an update) and a hook site. Everything else — trunk, data, loop, evaluator, controls — is shared, and that is the whole point.

  1. Subclass GeneratedUpdateCodecin src/adapterbench/t2p/codecs.py: output_size, apply(...), dense_delta(...), and initial_bias() (only if apply is bilinear in the generated params).
  2. Register itadd one entry to the constructors dict in make_codec, and a manifest under configs/adapters/.
  3. Run each settingat the codec's own best free hyperparameters over ≥3 seeds, via the setting's reproduce script.
  4. Submit a leaderboard rowthe shape, its free HPs, matched − control ± std, seed count, and the exact reproduce command.

See GUIDE.md for the full metric definitions and the fair-comparison hyperparameter partition, and BENCHMARK_CONTRACT.md for the interface contract.
